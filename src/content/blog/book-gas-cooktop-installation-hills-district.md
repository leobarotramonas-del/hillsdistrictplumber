A cooktop can fit the hole in the benchtop without being ready to connect. Before we discuss installation, we need to understand the appliance, gas supply and the space around it. Matching the advertised width is only one part of planning the job.

If you are comparing appliances, contact our [Hills District gas fitting team](/services/gas-fitting/) with the model information before committing to a purchase. We can discuss what needs assessing and which parts of the kitchen work require separate arrangements. Here is what makes that conversation useful.

### Send Us the Model, Not Just the Width

Send us the model specifications and installation instructions for the cooktop you are considering. We need the requirements for that particular appliance, not just a retailer’s description. Explain the existing arrangement as well, including whether the cooktop is staying in the same position.

The [NSW Government guide to using a gasfitter](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/building-or-renovating-a-home/preparing/using-a-gasfitter) explains that gas repair and renovation work must be completed by a licensed gasfitter. It also advises checking the gas supply type before buying an appliance.

Ask about the practical details:

- Is the proposed appliance suitable for the property's gas type?
- What installation information does the gasfitter need?
- Can the existing connection arrangement be assessed?
- Are changes to cabinets or the benchtop involved?
- Will another trade be needed for associated work?
- What access is required around and below the appliance?

Do not disconnect the old cooktop or alter its connection to help prepare for the visit.

### A Photo Helps, but It Is Not an Installation Assessment

Photos can help us understand the current layout and plan a visit. They cannot confirm everything about the installation. Keep the brand, model and specifications with those photos so we can discuss the proposed appliance alongside the existing setup.

Tell the team whether this is a replacement in the same position, a new connection or part of a kitchen renovation. Relocating the cooktop may involve a different scope from changing an appliance within an existing installation.

Customers in [Castle Hill](/service-areas/castle-hill/) should mention apartment or strata access where relevant. For [Baulkham Hills properties](/service-areas/baulkham-hills/), parking, cabinet access and any previous kitchen alterations are also useful booking details. Coverage and attendance are confirmed after the address and job information are reviewed.

### What Does “Install the Cooktop” Actually Include?

Before work proceeds, ask the gasfitter to explain the proposed connection work, any alterations identified during assessment and what checking will be completed. The agreed scope should make clear which parts of the job are included.

Useful questions include:

- Does the work include disconnection of the existing appliance?
- Who arranges removal of the old cooktop?
- Are additional fittings or pipe alterations expected?
- What happens if the installation differs from the supplied photos?
- Which associated tasks need separate arrangements?
- How will the completed work and documentation be handed over?

A clear scope reduces uncertainty when comparing proposals. The lowest starting figure is not necessarily a comparison of the same work, particularly when access or alterations have not yet been assessed.

### Who Is Handling the Rest of the Kitchen?

A kitchen project can combine plumbing, gas, electrical work, cabinetry and surface alterations. Confirm who is responsible for each part before booking several trades into the same space.

If a sink or dishwasher connection also needs attention, describe it when discussing [household plumbing repairs](/services/plumbing-repairs/). This helps the team understand the overall job without assuming every kitchen task is included in the gas-fitting scope.

For a gas hot-water appliance rather than a cooktop, see our [hot water repairs and replacement guide](/blog/hot-water-repair-or-replacement-hills-district/). The household demand, appliance location and installation considerations differ, so the booking information should reflect the actual system.

### Keep the Completion Documents with the Appliance

Use the [Service NSW tradesperson licence check](https://www.service.nsw.gov.au/transaction/check-a-builder-or-tradesperson-licence) to review the contractor's details and relevant work categories. Ask who will carry out the gas work if several trades are attending.

NSW Government guidance explains that completed gas work requires the appropriate compliance or inspection certificate, depending on the gas supply. Ask the gasfitter which document applies and retain it with the appliance instructions and other job records.

### Treat a Suspected Gas Leak as an Emergency

This article concerns planned installation. If you smell gas inside a property, leave immediately and call 000 from a safe location, following the NSW Government guidance linked above. Avoid flames and operating electrical switches in the affected area. Do not attempt to find or repair a leak yourself.
